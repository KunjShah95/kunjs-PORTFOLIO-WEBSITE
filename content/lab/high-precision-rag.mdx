## What I was testing

The obvious way to improve a retrieval-backed answer is to retrieve more.
Raise `k`, widen the window, let the model see everything and sort it out. The
competing hypothesis is that most retrieval failures are *precision* failures —
the right chunk is in the top 20 but not in the top 4 — and that a verification
pass can recover those without paying for the extra context.

## Method

Two arms over the same corpus and the same questions.

- **Arm A — more context.** Increase `k` until the answer stops improving. This
  sets the baseline cost of the naive fix.
- **Arm B — verify.** Keep `k` small, add a pass that checks each retrieved
  claim against its source and drops unsupported ones before generation.

Cost is measured in tokens actually consumed, not wall-clock, because that is
what determines whether the approach is viable at volume.

## Result

Partial. The constraint that came out of this is already shipping in
EngineerOS — *no source, no claim* — and it demonstrably removed the confident
hallucination failure mode. The measurement I set out to make is still open:
at equal token cost, a verification pass has not beaten a larger context window
on the questions I care about, and on short answers the larger window wins
because verification is pure overhead.

The honest read is that verification pays for itself when the *cost of being
wrong* is high, and does not when it is low. That is a more useful result than
the one I was hoping for.

## Still open

Whether a cheap verifier — a small model scoring claim-source support, rather
than a full second generation — closes the gap. That is the version that could
actually win.
