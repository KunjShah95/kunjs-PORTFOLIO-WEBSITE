## What I was testing

Quantisation is normally presented as a free 2x. I suspected it was not free for
a detector whose entire job is finding small, low-contrast objects — exactly
the kind of signal that lives in the low bits.

## Method

Two arms, same weights, same training data, same evaluation set.

- **Post-training quantisation.** Take the trained FP16 model and quantise it.
  No retraining. This is the free 2x.
- **Quantisation-aware training.** Continue training the final epochs with
  quantisation in the loop, so the weights adapt to the precision budget they
  will actually run under.

Both exported to the same TensorRT engine on the same device, so the only
variable is how the weights were prepared.

## Result

Post-training quantisation cost real accuracy on small defects, as predicted.
Quantisation-aware training held 98.7% detection accuracy while halving
inference time — 2x over FP16.

The mechanism is the interesting part. The defects that get quantised away are
the ones with low activation magnitude. During QAT the network learns to
represent them in the bits it will have, which is not something that can be
recovered after the fact.

## What it changed

Precision is not a post-hoc deployment setting. It is a training decision, and
making it after training means accepting whatever the lower precision destroys.
