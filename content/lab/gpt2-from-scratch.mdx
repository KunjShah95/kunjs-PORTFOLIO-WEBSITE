## What I was testing

Whether reading the paper is sufficient. I suspected not, and I suspected the
gap would be in the parts described in a sentence — the masking, the
initialisation scaling — rather than in the parts with equations.

## Method

Built GPT-2 small (124M) from the paper with NumPy and PyTorch. No HuggingFace,
no `transformers` library. Byte-pair tokenisation, learned embeddings with
weight tying, multi-head causal self-attention, GELU feed-forward blocks,
LayerNorm, the residual stream, and autoregressive generation with temperature,
top-k and nucleus sampling.

## Result

Attention was the hard part in the way I expected and not for the reason I
expected. Causal masking is one line of arithmetic and took longer to get right
than the entire feed-forward and normalisation path, because a transposed mask
produces plausible text and wrong gradients.

The residual stream was the surprise. It is two additions and it is the reason
deep transformers train at all.

The genuinely hidden hyperparameter was initialisation. Scaling non-embedding
weights by `0.02 / sqrt(num_layers)` is a single line that appears in a config
file as a number, and without it residual variance grows exponentially with
depth. Nobody explains why that number is what it is.

## What it changed

How I read papers. After implementing a component, references to variants of it
stop being trivia and start being legible. The cost was a few weeks; the
payoff has been every transformer paper since.
