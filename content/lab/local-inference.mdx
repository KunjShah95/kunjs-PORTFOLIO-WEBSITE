## What I was testing

Whether the local-versus-hosted decision is really about capability, or about
how strictly you constrain the output. My prior was that local models are only
good for chat and useless for anything that needs structure.

## Method

Took the skill-extraction step from GAP Miner and ran it two ways against the
same corpus: a hosted frontier model with a schema described in the prompt, and
Llama 3 locally with the schema enforced by the parsing layer rather than
requested in prose.

## Result

0.92 F1 on skill extraction, locally. The gap against a hosted model was
narrower than expected and lived almost entirely in malformed output, not wrong
output — which is exactly the failure mode a strict parser can absorb. Once the
parser rejects and retries rather than coercing, most of the quality difference
disappeared.

Throughput is roughly 2x slower than the reference implementation, and about 2x
faster in wall-clock than a hosted round trip once batching is accounted for.
For a pipeline that runs thousands of times, fixed cost beats per-token cost.

## What it changed

Corpus construction moved entirely local. The hosted path stayed for
interactive, low-volume work where a stronger model is worth the latency.

## Still open

Whether the same result holds for genuinely open-ended extraction. It probably
does not. Strict schemas are what make local inference viable, and schemas are
what you have when you already know the shape of the answer.
