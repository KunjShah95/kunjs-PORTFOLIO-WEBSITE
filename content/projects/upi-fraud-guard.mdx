<Section n="01" id="overview" title="Overview" kicker="Problem · Context · What I built">

## Problem

UPI processes an enormous volume of transactions, and the fraudulent fraction
is vanishingly small. Rule-based systems flag amounts above a threshold, block
repeated attempts from an IP, and watch for impossible locations. Fraudsters
have learned all of that: they probe with small amounts, they fan out across
accounts, and they imitate legitimate behaviour closely enough to pass.

The systems that catch what the rules miss are also the systems that block
legitimate customers, and the cost of a false block is paid by a person who did
nothing wrong.

## Context

The two errors are not equally expensive and the system has to treat them
asymmetrically. Missing a fraud costs money. Blocking a genuine transaction
costs a customer's trust in the payment system, which is a much harder thing to
recover. Precision-recall behaviour, not ROC AUC, is the metric that reflects
the actual decision.

## What I built

A real-time scoring engine, not a batch model.

- **Engineered behavioural features** — amount velocity, merchant diversity,
  geolocation entropy, time-of-day and hour-of-week patterns, device
  fingerprints, and each account's divergence from its own historical baseline.
- **XGBoost** as the primary classifier, on features that mostly encode
  metadata patterns rather than raw values.
- **Configurable decision thresholds** so an operator can tune sensitivity to
  their own risk tolerance rather than accepting whatever the model defaults to.
- **Class-imbalance handling** via resampling, because fraud is well under
  0.1% of volume.

<Section n="02" id="system" title="System" kicker="Architecture · Data flow · Infrastructure">

## Architecture

The model produces a score. The decision does not belong to the model. Bands
above a review threshold and below a block threshold route to different
outcomes, and both thresholds are operator-controlled.

## Data flow

Transaction → feature extraction including the account's rolling behavioural
baseline → XGBoost score → threshold comparison → allow, monitor, or review →
feedback from review outcomes back into retraining.

That last arrow is the part that makes it a system rather than a model. Review
decisions are the only source of ground truth that matters, and a fraud model
that never consumes them degrades silently as fraud patterns shift.

## Infrastructure

Flask service, Scikit-learn for preprocessing, XGBoost for the classifier.
Scoring is a single forward pass and stays under 100ms, which is what allows it
to sit in the transaction path rather than beside it.

</Section>

<Section n="03" id="engineering" title="Engineering" kicker="Decisions · Tradeoffs · Failures">

## Important decisions

**Metadata beats raw values.** Amount alone is nearly uninformative because
fraudsters control it. The account's *deviation from its own behaviour* is
extremely informative, and that is a feature, not a model change.

**The model ranks; the operator decides.** Configurable thresholds exist
because a payments operator knows their customers and their risk appetite, and
an ML engineer does not. A hard-coded threshold embeds an assumption about
whose losses matter and hides it.

**Evaluate on precision-recall.** With a positive rate this extreme, ROC AUC
looks excellent regardless of whether the system is usable. Precision-recall
surfaces the truth immediately.

## Tradeoffs

- **Resampling vs. calibrated probability.** Resampling fixes the training
  signal and damages the probability calibration. That is acceptable here
  because the model is used for ranking, and the thresholds are set by hand
  anyway.
- **Feature richness vs. overfitting.** Divergence features are powerful and
  can encode the training period's fraud pattern specifically, which then fails
  when the pattern changes.
- **Speed vs. feature depth.** Every additional feature costs latency in the
  transaction path, and the budget is tight.

## Failures

The model performed well offline and degraded in production because the feature
distribution shifted. The behavioural-baseline features were capturing the
specific fraud pattern present in the training window rather than a general
property of fraud, and it stopped being true a few weeks later.

The feedback loop from review outcomes was missing entirely at first, so
nothing detected the decay. Wiring review decisions back into retraining is
what turned a model into a system.

</Section>

<Section n="04" id="result" title="Result" kicker="What worked · What changed · What I learned">

## What worked

88% detection of anomalous transactions at under 0.1% false positives, around
85% precision, scoring in under 100ms.

## What changed

The mental model of the threshold. It started as a model output and became an
operator decision, which is both more honest and more useful.

## What I learned

- Precision-recall curves matter more than ROC AUC for fraud detection, because
  ROC AUC hides exactly the failure that makes a system unusable.
- Feature engineering on metadata patterns catches fraud that raw-value features
  miss completely.
- A fraud model without a feedback loop from review outcomes is a model with an
  expiry date.

</Section>
