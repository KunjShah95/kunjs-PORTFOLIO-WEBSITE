<Section n="01" id="overview" title="Overview" kicker="Problem · Context · What I built">

## Problem

Career planning is done by reading. Someone reads a hundred job descriptions,
extracts the skills mentally, compares them against their own, and produces a
list. It takes about ten days and it is done differently every time, so it
cannot be checked.

## Context

The reason it takes ten days is not the reading. It is that job descriptions
express the same skill in different words, so keyword extraction produces a
comparison of vocabularies rather than a comparison of skills. *Kubernetes*,
*container orchestration* and *workload scheduling on compute clusters* are one
requirement written three ways, and only one of them is a keyword.

## What I built

A pipeline that puts both sides of the comparison into the same semantic
space.

- **Structured skill extraction** with a local Llama 3 model via LangChain,
  producing normalised skill entities rather than raw phrases.
- **Vectorised market corpus** — job descriptions embedded into ChromaDB so
  similarity is computed over meaning.
- **Gap analysis** — the difference between a user's extracted skills and the
  skills demanded by the market corpus, weighted by demand frequency.

<Section n="02" id="system" title="System" kicker="Architecture · Data flow · Infrastructure">

## Architecture

Extraction and embedding are separate stages with separate failure modes, and
keeping them separate is what made each one debuggable. Extraction produces
normalised skill entities. Embedding is mechanical. Gap analysis is arithmetic
over the result.

## Data flow

Job description corpus → Llama 3 extraction into normalised skills → embedding
into ChromaDB → user profile extraction with the same pipeline → similarity
search of the profile against the corpus → demand-weighted difference →
ordered roadmap.

## Infrastructure

Local Llama 3 inference behind a FastAPI service. Local rather than hosted
because this pipeline runs constantly during corpus construction and a hosted
call per description does not scale economically.

</Section>

<Section n="03" id="engineering" title="Engineering" kicker="Decisions · Tradeoffs · Failures">

## Important decisions

**Local inference for the hot path.** Corpus extraction is thousands of calls.
A local model makes the cost effectively fixed and removes a rate limit from
the critical path. It also requires real prompt discipline to get usable
structured output.

**One pipeline for both sides of the comparison.** Using different extraction
logic for the profile and the corpus would introduce an asymmetry that shows up
as a phantom gap. Both sides run through the identical normaliser, which is why
the alignment figure is as high as it is.

**Demand weighting, not set difference.** A skill that appears in two
percent of postings is not a gap. Weighting each requirement by its frequency
in the corpus is what turns a set difference into a prioritised roadmap.

## Tradeoffs

- **Local model capability vs. cost.** A local model is weaker at structured
  extraction than a frontier one, and the 0.92 F1 is the visible consequence.
  For a pipeline that runs thousands of times, that is the right trade.
- **Normalised entities vs. nuance.** Normalising *container orchestration* and
  *Kubernetes* into one entity loses the distinction that might matter for a
  specific role. Preserving both costs the F1.
- **Corpus freshness vs. cost.** Rebuilding the corpus is expensive. It is
  rebuilt on a cadence, which means the answer is correct as of the last build.

## Failures

The first extraction pass returned skills as the phrases they appeared in, so
the comparison was between vocabularies and the reported gaps were mostly
synonyms. Forcing normalised entities through the schema fixed the whole
class of problem.

</Section>

<Section n="04" id="result" title="Result" kicker="What worked · What changed · What I learned">

## What worked

Roadmap generation in about fifteen seconds instead of ten days, at 95% market
alignment, with skill extraction F1 of 0.92. The speed is the headline; the
alignment is what makes it useful.

## What changed

The framing from "read and compare" to "normalise, embed, difference". The
second is checkable.

## What I learned

- Semantic similarity over embeddings beats keyword matching for anything where
  the same requirement is written in different words — which is most job
  descriptions.
- Local LLMs handle structured extraction at a fraction of hosted cost, and the
  quality gap is manageable when the schema is strict.
- The same extractor must run on both sides, or you are measuring your own
  inconsistency.

</Section>
