Fraudsters are getting better. Static rules cannot keep up with them, and this
is the story of a machine learning system that catches what the rules miss
without blocking the people who did nothing wrong.

## The problem

UPI processes an enormous volume of transactions and the fraudulent fraction is
vanishingly small. Traditional detection flags amounts above a threshold, blocks
repeated attempts from an IP, watches for impossible locations. Fraudsters
learned all of that — they probe with small amounts, fan out across accounts,
and imitate legitimate behaviour closely enough to pass.

## The asymmetry

The two errors are not equally expensive.

Missing fraud costs money. Blocking a genuine transaction costs a customer
their trust in the payment system, which is much harder to recover. Every
subsequent decision followed from taking that asymmetry seriously — including
that ROC AUC is the wrong metric, because it looks excellent here regardless of
whether the system is usable.

## What I built

XGBoost over engineered behavioural features: amount velocity, merchant
diversity, geolocation entropy, time-of-day patterns, device fingerprints, and
each account's divergence from its own historical baseline.

That last feature is the important one. Amount alone is nearly uninformative
because fraudsters control it. The account's *deviation from its own behaviour*
is extremely informative, and that is a feature, not a model change.

The model produces a score. The decision does not belong to the model —
thresholds are operator configuration, because a payments operator knows their
customers and their risk appetite, and an ML engineer does not.

## The failure that mattered

It performed well offline and degraded in production because the feature
distribution shifted. The behavioural features were capturing the specific
fraud pattern present in the training window rather than a general property of
fraud, and it stopped being true a few weeks later.

Worse, nothing detected the decay, because review decisions were not feeding
back into retraining. A fraud model without that loop has an expiry date.

## The result

88% detection of anomalous transactions at under 0.1% false positives, ~85%
precision, scoring in under 100ms.

**The best fraud detection is invisible. It stops bad transactions without the
good ones ever noticing.**
