The idea came from a conversation. A friend had received a job offer that
looked great on paper — good salary, nice title, respectable company. But
something felt off, and they could not put their finger on it. They asked me:
*can you build something that reads this and tells me what to look out for?*

I said yes before I knew how.

## The problem

Job offers are documents designed to look good. The recruiter is on your side
right up until they are not. Ghost jobs, bait-and-switch titles, burnout culture
hidden behind *fast-paced* and *wear many hats*.

The existing tools were either generic — anonymous sentiment from strangers —
or expensive. Nobody was reading the actual text of the offer.

## What I built

Three inputs — job description, offer letter, recruiter chat — analysed
together across five dimensions: toxicity signals, burnout risk, salary
fairness, ghost job detection, and negotiation strategy.

The technical shape is a three-stage model chain. A cheap primary scanner
triages the document and marks regions of interest. A deep analyser extracts
structured signals from just those regions. A cross-check model re-reads the
result looking for internal inconsistency.

Running all three over the full document would be three times the cost for a
worse answer.

## The hard part was not technical

When you tell someone their job offer might be toxic, you are affecting a real
decision in their life. I spent more time on the disclaimer than on any single
feature.

I also learned that people do not want a verdict. They want context. The most
useful output is not the red-flag count — it is the specific sentence,
highlighted in the original text, with an explanation of why it matters. A score
invites someone to obey a number. A highlighted sentence invites them to read.

That is the difference between a system that informs a decision and one that
launders its own uncertainty into false confidence.

## The result

Live, no signup, nothing stored. The feedback that matters is when someone
writes back saying *that helped me negotiate a better offer.*

**That is why I build things.**
