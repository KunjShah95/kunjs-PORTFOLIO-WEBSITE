A railway track inspection system has to find small defects in metal, at speed,
in daylight and pouring rain, on a computer that fits in a backpack. This is how
I built one.

## The problem

Manual inspection is slow, dangerous and inconsistent. A human walks miles
looking for cracks, deformations and wear patterns. From a moving train the
window to see a defect is milliseconds.

Existing automated systems were expensive and needed specialised hardware. I
wanted something that would run on affordable edge hardware and still meet the
accuracy requirement.

## The constraint that decided everything

Sub-100ms end to end — frame ingestion, preprocessing, inference and
post-processing combined. Not on a workstation. On a fanless Jetson Orin
sitting at the edge.

That one number determined the architecture. On an A100 the problem is
uninteresting; on hardware with no fans, the model is the least interesting part.

## What I built

YOLOv8 with a four-stage pipeline: hardware-accelerated GStreamer decode, CUDA
preprocessing, a TensorRT INT8 engine compiled for the Orin, and post-processing
on the host.

The moves that mattered:

- **Python out, C++ in.** A per-frame inference loop on an edge device is
  bottlenecked by the language. TensorRT with a device-specific engine was worth
  about 3x on its own.
- **Quantisation-aware training, not post-training quantisation.** Naive INT8
  quantisation quietly destroys small-defect accuracy — small low-contrast
  objects are exactly what gets quantised away. Letting the weights adapt to
  the precision budget during the final epochs held 98.7% while halving
  inference time.
- **Fix the environment before blaming the model.** Motion blur and low light
  both look identical to model failure. Handling them upstream recovered
  accuracy without touching the weights.

## The result

98.7% detection accuracy, 4.2x faster than manual inspection, under 100ms end
to end, on affordable edge hardware. INT8 gave 2x over FP16 with accuracy
intact.

That last number is the difference between a lab demo and something that could
be deployed.

**The edge is not about the model. It is about every millisecond.**
